# 06 — Packing Templates

*Capsule logic: minimum pieces, maximum combinations.*

**The method: one shoe family, one trouser family, one colour spine.** Everything packed must combine with everything else packed. If a piece only works with one other piece, it doesn't go in the bag.

**The spine is warm-neutral:** navy or charcoal below, cream / olive / tobacco above, brown shoes throughout. That single decision is what makes three days' clothing fit in a carry-on.

**Always in the bag:** #117 Nudie cream sweater *(the flying piece)* · #163 olive/brown scarf *(doubles as a plane wrap)* · #20 sunglasses · #160 chain.

**Check the `ring_fence` column before packing anything.**

### The airline table

*Unexpanded the Tumi is 23 cm deep. That depth is what passes or fails.*

| Airline | Cabin allowance | #175 unexpanded |
|---|---|---|
| **British Airways** | 56 × 45 × 25, 23 kg, **plus** a 40 × 30 × 15 personal item | ✅ **Clears comfortably.** #168 goes as the second bag |
| **Jet2** | 56 × 45 × 25, 10 kg, plus a personal item | ✅ Clears on size. **10 kg cap** |
| **easyJet** *(paid large cabin bag)* | 56 × 45 × 25, 15 kg | ✅ Clears |
| **Wizz Air** *(Priority)* | 55 × 40 × 23, 10 kg | ⚠️ **Exactly at the limit on all three dimensions.** No margin |
| **Ryanair** *(Priority)* | 55 × 40 × 20, 10 kg | ❌ **FAILS by 3 cm of depth.** It will not go in the sizer |

**Three consequences, not negotiable:**

1. **Never expand it for a cabin leg.** At 28 cm it busts every allowance including BA's. The expansion is for a checked leg or a drive.
2. **On Ryanair the Tumi is a checked bag.** There is no cabin version. **The cabin-only alternative is #168 as the Priority bag** — it clears 55 × 40 × 20 easily, but *not* the free 40 × 25 × 20 small bag, so Priority is a purchase either way. 12.5 L is a two-night ceiling and only if the bulk is worn.
3. **The 4.2 kg tare eats the budget airlines.** Against a 10 kg cap that leaves **under 6 kg** for clothing, shoes and washbag. Template A makes it; Template C does not.

*BA is the airline this wardrobe is really built around — 23 kg plus the Rains means weight never binds. The table is what happens when the trip isn't on BA.*

## Standing rules

1. **Roll knitwear and tees, fold shirts over a jacket.** The jacket goes in last, shoulders out.
2. **Never pack the heaviest coat — wear it.**
3. **The belt matches the leather nearest it** — the shoes in leather shoes, the **watch strap** in trainers. **On a blue or black sport band the belt is orphaned and comes off.**
4. **Socks matched to trousers, not shoes.** Compromised until charcoal and navy arrive; no-shows sidestep it in summer.
5. **Everything packed must go with everything else packed.** No orphan pieces.
6. **Check the destination's weather**, not London's.
7. **The expansion is for checked legs and drives only.**
8. **Check the airline before the wardrobe.** On Ryanair and Wizz the bag decides the packing list, not the other way round.
9. **Check the `ring_fence` column.**
10. **If a named item is unavailable, substitute by slot** — same layer, same colour role (light half or dark half), same or higher formality rank. **Never drop the slot.** These templates name specific garments, so a retirement or a repair would otherwise break a template silently.
11. **Every packed piece appears in the day map at least once, and every open layer in the map names the piece under it.** The map is the proof that rule 5 holds — a pack list alone cannot show it. **An open layer is never a free choice of base:** field rule 1 in `00-rules.md` decides which bases are legal, and an unnamed base is an unchecked one. A piece that survives the map with no appearance comes out of the bag. **The map is also what catches what is missing** — a shoe with no belt, an evening with no sock — so build it before the trip, not at the airport.

## What would most improve every template

**A packable rain shell** · **a soft weekender** · **two merino crews** · **charcoal and navy socks** · **a second pair of swim shorts** · **a garment bag** · **two plain short-sleeve shirts, olive and cream.**

All but the weekender and the garment bag are small money, and every one appears in more than one template.
