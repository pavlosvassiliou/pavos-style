## B. Style identity

**Default silhouette: properly tailored and sharp.** Close to the body, structured shoulder, nothing loose.

**One exception: t-shirts are worn RELAXED** — the 220gsm cut, not the 170. Check the hem sits around mid-fly; if it covers the seat it cuts the leg line.

**Hard no-gos:** visible logos or branding · loud pattern · skinny or cropped trousers · scratchy or synthetic fabrics (sportswear excepted).

**Material taste:** sealed, uniform leather reads cheap to him. **Prefers visible grain that patinas** — Horween Chromexcel, vegetable-tanned, full-grain pull-up. Applies to belts, straps, bags and wallets.

**The buying rule: check the shoulder first.** If a jacket's shoulder isn't within about a centimetre of **41.5**, stop reading. **Quote centimetres, not UK sizes,** wherever a brand offers the choice.

**Fit quirks:** sleeves long, trousers long, jackets boxy at the waist — the signature of a lean 1.64 m frame buying garments drafted for 1.78 m. **A length problem, not a fit problem.** Buy to the shoulder and budget alterations into every jacket and trouser.

### The three contested numbers

- **Seat 54 is less than thigh 55**, which is impossible — the seat goes round both legs and the pelvis. Expect **88–95**.
- **Trouser waist 86 contradicts the belt.** #129 is a size 30 with holes spanning roughly 71–81 cm and it fastens comfortably.
- **Chest-to-waist drop is 4.5 cm**, where a lean 65 kg frame shows 15–20. If the waist is wrong the drop resolves and the chest is fine.

> **Two five-minute garment checks settle all three without a body tape.** Measure **#129** from the buckle prong to the hole actually used. Measure **#49** flat across the top of the waistband, ×2. **If they come back near 76–80, the 86 was a bad reading.**

### The foot-length note

25.0 cm reads as roughly **UK 6.5–7**, yet UK 6 fits three ways. Both can be true — a close-fitting shoe in leather that gives. **But when a brand publishes centimetres, quote 25.0 and take their recommendation.**

### The tailor's list — six measurements, in centimetres

1. **Shoulder**, bony point to bony point across the back
2. **Nape to hip bone** — the Short-jacket rule as a number
3. **Nape to top of kneecap** — the coat ceiling
4. **Jacket sleeve**, nape over the shoulder to the wrist bone
5. **Shirt sleeve**, shoulder point to wrist bone
6. **Inseam to no break** — every trouser bought from here gets hemmed to this

**Ask for #6 twice — once in the Axel Arigatos (#70), once in the Gianvito Rossi boots (#108)** — and take both pairs. A boot sits higher, and knowing both stops a hem being right with sneakers and wrong with boots. **Also ask them to settle the waist and seat.**

### Garment benchmarks worth taking

*Measure the things that already fit — those numbers become the spec a product page gets checked against. Lay flat, buttoned, smoothed not stretched; chest is pit-to-pit.*

## E. Practicalities

**Budget — buy well, buy once.** Knitwear £150–250 · shirting £120–200 · trousers £150–250 · shoes £350–500 · outerwear £500–800 · blazers £400–700.

**Laundry — one wash a week, handled by someone else.** Needs 7–10 days of clean smart-casual in circulation. The wash rule is in `03-state.md`; there is no per-item wear counter, deliberately.

**Upkeep and storage are not constraints.** Laundry, ironing and dry-cleaning are handled — linen, crisp cotton, unlined tailoring, fine wool and merino are all viable.

**Underwear:** Calvin Klein, trunk cut, white or black. CDLP being phased out.

**Tan cycle:** tans easily, deepens a lot over summer. The seasonal palette shift is in `00-rules.md`.
