## How this file has been wrong before

**Three failure modes, all still live risks.**

1. **Derivation from a two-part colour string.** *"Dark grey / faded black"* → `hue = grey`. The machine took the first half. **Nine items ended up in the wrong colour family.**
2. **Photo reads under artificial light.** A brown-looking shirt that is light grey; an olive crochet that photographs brown. **A single-look photo read is provenance B and never overwrites anything.**
3. **Inference from a brand catalogue.** The Manebi espadrilles were "corrected" to Carbon Grey because *"Manebi has no blue-grey"*. **They are navy.** What a brand's range contains is not evidence about a specific object.

**And the thing that breaks provenance E:** confirming an auto-derived field without examining the object. A guess promoted to *confirmed* is worse than a guess that admits it.

## Why there are no typed counts

Five documents once carried five different record counts, three carried a face-garment census that had omitted a garment, and the packing templates named garments by colours that had been disproved eight days earlier.

The lesson — *a count quoted in prose goes stale the moment a row is added* — had been **written into the project and not enforced**, and three more documents went stale in the following week.

**The fix was not more diligence. It was removing the ability to make the mistake:** one owner per fact, no typed counts, and ring-fences moved from prose into a column.

**What the `ring_fence` column was added to prevent, in one case:** Template A in `06-packing.md` carried a paragraph excluding #24 Soloio and restating that it is navy, RELAXED and casual-and-weekend-only — three CSV attributes retyped as prose, standing beside a rule that already says to check the column. Redundant the day the column landed, and free to drift from it. Removed 30 Aug 2026.

## The capture method, and why it stopped

The plan was once to photograph roughly 70 items through a purpose-built app. **That was wrong and it was slow** — most of what made the database inaccurate was not a photo problem.

| Broken | Fix | Needed a camera? |
|---|---|---|
| `colour` | Look at it in daylight and say the colour | **No** |
| `fabric` | Read the care label aloud | **No** |
| `fit` / `hem` | A worn photograph | **Yes** — 15 outerwear items, still outstanding |

**Stopped:** photographing every item · tapping "Correct" on auto-derived fabric · iterating on the app · treating accessories as pending.

**Photographs were the method. They were never the point.**
