# 00 — The Rules

*The single rulebook. If a rule isn't here, it isn't a rule.*

**Who this dresses:** Pavlos · 1.64 m, lean · warm-neutral olive skin · near-black hair with heavy silver · **high value contrast, low colour contrast** · London.

---

## The three laws

1. **Dress in value steps.** One clearly light piece and one clearly dark, or full tonal monochrome. Mid-tones head to toe read as fog.
2. **One unbroken vertical.** Nothing cuts the body horizontally. Outranks colour, outranks fashion.
3. **Warm by day, black by night.** Olive, tobacco, camel and cream at the collar on working days — in serious fabric. Black near the face is evening-only, and only with texture.

**Law 1 is read above the ankle.** The light and the dark must both sit in the clothed body — outerwear, mid, base, trouser. **A light shoe never supplies the light half:** under a dark leg it reads as a break in the line, not a value step. Footwear answers to the proportion doctrine, not to law 1.

**A forced look is labelled.** When the available set can't satisfy a law — away list, dirty list, a thin bench — say so on the line and name the law it breaks. A compromise presented as clean is worse than not offering the look at all.

## The colouring

| | |
|---|---|
| Skin | Light-to-medium **olive**, warm-neutral with a green cast. Takes colour rather than reflecting it |
| Hair | Near-black with pronounced silver-white throughout |
| Eyes | Warm mid-brown, amber flecks, darker olive-green outer ring |
| Undertone | **Warm-neutral olive.** Very warm yellow-based colours turn the skin sallow |
| Contrast | **High value, low colour.** He carries strong dark-to-light jumps that would overwhelm a softer-coloured man, and nothing in his colouring clashes with anything else |

## The palette

- **CORE** — navy/ink · charcoal *(the daytime dark)* · cream, ecru, oatmeal · optic white *(works because of the high contrast)* · camel, tan, warm stone · **olive and deep sage — the best family, core not accent**
- **STRONG, buy first** — tobacco, chocolate, warm mid-brown · deep teal/petrol · oxblood *(evening and knits)* · rust *(knits only, never jackets)*
- **CONDITIONAL** — black: below the waist and on the feet freely, near the face evening-and-texture only · bright red and royal blue: deep versions, accent only
- **DEAD** — pastels · dusty muted mid-tones · icy and cool-silver tones · mustard and warm brights · coral/salmon · neon
- **UNPROVEN** — **deep forest green.** The validation references in forest didn't land where olive and sage did. **#172 is the test in a garment already owned: wear it once and decide**

**Grey:** charcoal is STRONG anywhere · light grey is OK · **mid grey marl FAILS at the collar** — his hair is already doing mid-grey and a marl merges with it.

**Seasonal shift.** The skin deepens over summer while the hair stays constant, so value contrast drops in summer and rises in winter. The palette doesn't change; the emphasis does.

- **Summer, tanned** — cream, ecru, optic white, camel and tan peak. Rust moves from strong to excellent. **Head-to-toe cream is viable in summer and nowhere else**
- **Winter, paler** — deep colours near the face do the work. **Prefer cream and ecru over optic white.** The one-light-one-dark rule becomes strict

**Metals: both, deliberately.** Warm metals against the skin — rings, bracelets, buckles, straps. Cool metals echo the hair and read modern. **Default: steel case on a brown leather strap.**

## The proportion doctrine

*Everything scales down to 1.64 m. The objective of every outfit is an unbroken vertical line. This outranks colour.*

| Rule | Why |
|---|---|
| **No turn-ups. Ever.** | A hard horizontal across the shin |
| **No break, or a bare kiss of one** | Pooling fabric eats leg length |
| **Jacket length: Short, always** — hem at the hip bone | A standard R ends mid-thigh and halves the body |
| **Coats end at the top of the kneecap** | Floor: must still cover the jacket beneath by 1–2″ |
| **Mid-to-high rise trousers** | A low rise starts the leg lower |
| **Untucked hem at the top of the fly**, never past the seat | — |
| **Sock matches the TROUSER, never the shoe** | Carries colour past the ankle |
| **Trouser, sock and shoe in related tones** | — |
| **Belt quiet, matched, or absent.** Beltless with light summer trousers; never brown on black | A plate buckle cuts him in half |
| **Chelsea and chukka boots over derbies** | A boot continues the trouser line |
| **Single-strap or hand-carried bags over backpacks** | Two straps add bulk across the shoulders |
| **Narrow lapels · smaller collars · medium-scale sunglasses · slim cardholder** | Scale everything down |
| **Watch case 38–41 mm** | The owned 42 mm is grandfathered, not a precedent. A future dress watch: 36–38 mm steel, brown strap |
| **Tie 6.5–7 cm, four-in-hand, tip at the buckle** | — |
| **No motif bigger than his palm** | — |
| **Roll the sleeves** on long-sleeve shirts in casual and holiday | — |
| **Buy to the shoulder, tailor the rest** | The shoulder is the only unalterable dimension |

## The five modes

| Mode | Register | Rules |
|---|---|---|
| **Client day** | Sharp and modern, tieless | Jacket-led · open collar · **warm at the collar** · shirts tucked, tees untucked · denim rarely · watch and white pocket square |
| **Dinner** | Black-led, tucked | A jacket in every look · black correct **with texture** · full tonal darks |
| **Casual day** | Seen, not working | Chore jacket or overshirt over tee or knit · untucked · sleeves rolled · white sneakers or loafers |
| **Off duty** | Uniform mode | Fixed set, never generated. Fitted knit + dark jean |
| **Holiday** | Two garments, one layer | Rolled linen · untucked · no belt · cream and sand lead · brown leather on the feet · no prints |

**Dinner is achromatic and that is correct** — black is his evening colour, charcoal his daytime dark. **Client day is where the discipline is needed:** warm at the collar. Holiday is already right. Below the waist and outerwear, achromatic freely.

**The reason client day needs discipline:** warm reads as *leisure* to him and achromatic reads as *serious*. That association is context, not taste — olive, tobacco and camel read serious the moment they arrive in fine merino, tailoring and leather. **The colours were never the holiday; the fabric was.**

**Black tie:** #23 Etro purple velvet with a black bow tie is a proven move. Jewel-tone velvet is established alternative dress.

## Fabric law

- **Black needs pile.** Velvet and corduroy near the face for evening; flat cotton no; below the waist always fine. **A melange or marl earns the same exemption as pile.**
- **Crease reads leisure, drape reads serious.** Linen is holiday. Merino and wool make the best colours read professional.
- **Leather shows its grain.** Visible grain, pull-up, patina. Sealed uniform leather reads cheap to him.

## The purchase filter — all eight, or it stays in the shop

Solid colour · in palette · no turn-ups · Short where sized · mid-to-high rise · no visible logo · pairs with ≥3 owned items · **fits the shoulder first, within ~1 cm of 41.5.**

**Standing colour rule:** every colour purchase is **olive, tobacco, cream or charcoal** until warm-at-the-collar stops being a minority of the face garments. **Not navy. Not blue. Not another white tee.**

---

## Field rules

*Learned from mirror checks and worn days. These outrank anything derived on paper.*

1. **Tonal same-hue layering is conditional on closure.** Two pieces in one hue family work *buttoned*, where only a collar strip shows. Worn **open** they sit side by side in the same plane and the near-miss in value reads as a mistake. **Open layer ⇒ contrast the base.**
2. **The belt matches the leather nearest it** — the shoes in leather shoes, the **watch strap** in trainers. **On a blue or black sport band the belt is orphaned and comes off.**

## Scope

**The terminal product is a locked set of outfits, not a style philosophy.**

> boards → rules → generated looks → sense check → tried on → **LOCKED**

A locked look is not revisited. Daily styling is *picking from the locked set*; generating from scratch is for novel occasions only.

**This rulebook is retained for three jobs:** the purchase filter · building a replacement when a look fails or an item retires · novel occasions. Anything else is scope creep and the answer is no.
